## 4. Value hypothesis
- They have publicly committed to AI upskilling but a university's own staff (professional services, marketing, recruitment, design teams) are themselves non-technical AI adopters — our AI training for non-technical teams maps directly to internal adoption, governance, and best-practice gaps that a research-led Academy may not cover for its own back-office.
- Proof point: we helped a client draw their AI org chart and hire/save roles 50% faster — directly relevant to an institution standing up new AI Academy/Lab functions and deciding which roles to build vs. buy.

## 5. Discovery questions
1. Coventry's AI Skills Academy targets the external workforce — how are you handling AI upskilling for your own internal teams (marketing, recruitment, professional services)?
2. As you stand up the AI Adoption Lab and Academy, how are you deciding which AI roles to hire versus reskill existing staff into?
3. What does "responsible" or governed AI use look like in practice for non-technical staff across the university right now?
4. Where are repetitive, manual tasks across your administrative or marketing teams that AI could take off people's plates first?
5. How do you measure whether an AI training or adoption effort is actually changing day-to-day work, not just awareness?
