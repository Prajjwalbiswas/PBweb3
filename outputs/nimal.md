- UCL just launched a new People Plan 2025/26–2027/28 focused on building "an adaptable workforce," career development, and improving digital platforms — a fresh, funded mandate to upskill ~18,000+ staff. (ucl.ac.uk, Jan 2026)

## 4. Value hypothesis
- UCL's own "future of work" framing and "adaptable workforce" People Plan point to a need to move large non-technical professional-services and academic-support teams from awareness to confident, safe day-to-day AI use — our core offering: AI adoption, automating repetitive tasks, governance, security and best practices for non-technical teams.
- Most relevant proof point: we helped a client map their AI org chart and hire/save roles 50% faster — directly relevant to a People Plan built around workforce adaptability and career-progression decisions.

## 5. Discovery questions
1. Following the January "AI and the Future of Work" event, what concrete next steps has UCL committed to for preparing your own staff (not just students) to work with AI?
2. The new People Plan calls for "an adaptable workforce" — how are you currently equipping non-technical professional-services teams to use AI safely in their day-to-day work?
3. Where are repetitive, manual tasks across admin, marketing or student-services teams that AI could realistically take off people's plates this year?
4. How is AI governance and security handled today when staff experiment with AI tools — is there a defined set of best practices people are trained on?
5. As roles shift with AI, how are you thinking about which capabilities to build internally versus hire for — and who owns that decision?
