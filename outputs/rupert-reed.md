# Godliman Partners — Discovery Brief
**Rupert Reed, Managing Director & Founder** · Prepared 2026-06-30

## 1. Snapshot (≤3 lines)
- Boutique executive search consultancy that recruits senior leaders and teams for asset management firms (public/private markets, sales & distribution, C-suite).
- Industry: executive search / talent (asset-management vertical) · size: ~11–50 staff · HQ: London, UK (Godliman House, 21 Godliman Street, EC4V 5BD).

## 2. Why now — the trigger
- The asset-management sector (Godliman's entire client base) is being pushed toward "AI-first" operating models in 2026 — e.g. BCG's *Global Asset Management Report 2026: Rebuilding Asset Management for an AI-First World* and Goldman Sachs rolling out AI-education programs to portfolio-company leaders. As an advisory-led search firm whose product is market insight (its weekly Press Digest reaches 5,000+ asset-management subscribers, and it publishes Reading Room hiring-trend briefings), Godliman has commercial reason to build its own team's AI fluency to keep advising clients credibly. [BCG 2026; Goldman GSAM 2025; godliman.com Reading Room]
- Most recent published insight, "The 'Invisibles' of High Quality Search" (2 May 2026), signals an active thought-leadership cadence — a natural hook for a conversation about equipping the non-technical research/consulting team with AI skills. [godliman.com, 2 May 2026]

## 4. Value hypothesis
- A small, non-technical team of researchers and consultants spends heavily on repetitive work — desk research, candidate market-mapping, drafting the weekly Press Digest and Reading Room briefings. Practical AI training would let the existing 11–50 people produce more research per head and adopt AI safely (governance/security matter when handling confidential client and candidate data).
- Most relevant proof point: we helped a client draw their AI org chart and hire/save roles 50% faster — directly resonant for a search firm that itself advises clients on org design and talent.

## 5. Discovery questions
1. As asset-management clients move toward "AI-first" operating models, how are they expecting their search and advisory partners to use AI in the work you deliver?
2. Today, how much of your consultants' and researchers' time goes into repeatable tasks like market-mapping, candidate research, and producing the Press Digest and Reading Room briefings?
3. Have any of your team started using AI tools on their own, and do you have any guidance or guardrails around using them with confidential client or candidate data?
4. When you think about your team's AI skills over the next year, who owns that — is it something you'd drive yourself, or share with Sarah and the senior consultants?
5. What would "good" look like 90 days after a team training program — more research output, faster turnarounds, better-quality insight, or something else?

## 6. Opener
"Rupert — with reports like BCG's 'AI-First World' piece reframing how asset managers expect to operate, I wanted to ask how you're thinking about getting Godliman's own research and consulting team AI-fluent, given how much of your edge is market insight."

## 7. Watch-outs (include ONLY if real)
- Small team (~11–50): budget and appetite for formal training may be limited; lead with productivity/ROI, not curriculum breadth.
- No public evidence of any existing AI training, tooling, or vendor relationship was found — treat the AI-adoption stage as Unknown and confirm in discovery rather than assuming greenfield.
- Confidential candidate/client data is core to the business — AI governance and security will likely be a gating concern, so lead with it as a strength.
