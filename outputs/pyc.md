# University of Bristol — Discovery Brief
**PYC, Unknown title · Prepared 2026-06-30**

## 1. Snapshot (≤3 lines)
- A large, research-intensive UK public university (founding Russell Group member, 1994) teaching and researching across all disciplines, now a national hub for AI compute and AI education.
- Higher education / research · ~27,000 students and ~8,000 staff · Bristol, UK ([source](https://www.russell-group.ac.uk/about/our-universities/university-of-bristol/)).

## 2. Why now — the trigger
- In January 2026 Bristol launched government-funded **Spärck AI scholarships** alongside **four new MSc programmes for 2026/27** — including **MSc Artificial Intelligence for Business** — signalling a major institutional push to scale AI capability beyond pure computer science ([source](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/news/2026/january/new-government-funded-ai-scholarships-launch-at-the-university-of-bristol.html), Jan 2026).
- Bristol hosts **Isambard-AI**, the most powerful university-based supercomputer in the world (11th fastest globally), fully live since June 2025 — a £225m facility creating intense demand to upskill non-research staff and teaching teams around AI use, governance, and best practice ([source](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/research/centres/bristol-supercomputing/articles/2025/isambard-ai-is-11th-fastest-supercomputer-in-the-world.html), 2025).

## 4. Value hypothesis
- Bristol is rapidly expanding AI infrastructure and curricula, which creates a gap in equipping non-technical teams (professional services, marketing/comms, admissions, design) to adopt AI safely — exactly the AI-adoption, automation, governance, and best-practice training we deliver.
- Proof point: we helped a client map their AI org chart and hire/save roles 50% faster — directly relevant to an institution standing up new AI programmes and needing to allocate roles and skills around them.

## 5. Discovery questions
1. With four new AI MSc programmes and Isambard-AI live, how are you currently equipping non-research and professional-services staff to use AI in their day-to-day work?
2. Who owns AI governance and acceptable-use policy across the university, and how is that being communicated to non-technical teams?
3. Beyond the BILT teaching pilots, is there a structured training path for marketing, admissions, or operations teams, or is adoption happening ad hoc?
4. As you hire and reshape roles around the new AI programmes and facilities, how are you deciding which skills to build internally versus recruit?
5. What does "good" look like 12 months from now for AI capability among your non-technical staff — and what's blocking it today?

## 6. Opener
- "With the new AI for Business MSc and Isambard-AI both live, you're clearly scaling AI fast on the research side — I wanted to ask how you're bringing your non-technical teams along at the same pace."

## 7. Watch-outs (include ONLY if real)
- BILT already runs internal AI teaching/learning pilots and publishes AI guidance, so there is in-house capability — position as complementary upskilling for non-technical/professional teams rather than duplicating academic work ([source](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/bilt/sharing-practice/guides/guidance-on-ai/our-commitment-to-the-russell-group-principles/)).
