# OpenAI — Discovery Brief
**Peter Steinberger, Engineer / AI Agents (joined Feb 2026)** · Prepared 2026-06-30

## 1. Snapshot (≤3 lines)
- OpenAI builds frontier AI models (ChatGPT, GPT series) and increasingly autonomous AI agents, sold to consumers and enterprises.
- Industry: AI / foundation models · Size: ~7,850–9,300 employees (est. mid-2026) · HQ: San Francisco, CA.

## 2. Why now — the trigger
- Peter joined OpenAI on 15 Feb 2026 (announced by Sam Altman) to **lead/build the next generation of personal AI agents** after his viral open-source project OpenClaw — a fresh mandate with a stated goal of agents "so simple even his mother could use it." A new-role + new-mission moment is the strongest reason to engage.
- His agent vision is explicitly about non-expert usability and AI "that actually does things" — directly adjacent to the enablement/adoption problem our training solves.

## 3. Who you're talking to
- **Peter Steinberger** — Engineer focused on personal AI agents at OpenAI; founder/former CEO of PSPDFKit (€100M Insight Partners deal, 2021) and creator of OpenClaw. He likely cares about making powerful AI usable by non-technical, everyday people — the core thesis of his current work. LinkedIn: https://www.linkedin.com/in/steipete/
- (No second stakeholder material to the approach; engage Peter directly.)

## 4. Value hypothesis
- Peter's mission is to make agents usable by non-experts; we make non-technical teams (design, sales, marketing) fluent and safe with AI — a natural philosophical match if he ever needs an external enablement partner for go-to-market or partner orgs adopting his agents.
- Proof point: we helped a client draw their AI org chart and hire/save roles 50% faster — relevant as agent-driven teams rethink which roles humans vs. agents own.
- Caveat: OpenAI is an AI-native company; the realistic fit is enablement/governance for non-technical functions or its ecosystem, not core engineering teams (see Watch-outs).

## 5. Discovery questions
1. As you design agents for non-experts, what have you learned about where ordinary users get stuck — and how much of that is a training/literacy gap vs. a product gap?
2. Inside OpenAI, how do non-technical functions (design, marketing, GTM) get up to speed on the agent tooling you're shipping?
3. When you think about OpenClaw's open-source community adopting agents, what support or best-practice gaps slow them down?
4. How are you thinking about governance and safe-use guardrails as agents start "doing things" autonomously on users' behalf?
5. From PSPDFKit to OpenClaw, you've scaled teams — where did non-technical staff most need structured AI upskilling rather than learning on the fly?

## 6. Opener
"Saw you joined OpenAI in February to build personal AI agents for everyone — the 'so simple my mother could use it' bar is exactly the non-technical adoption problem we work on; curious how you're thinking about the human-readiness side of that."

## 7. Watch-outs (include ONLY if real)
- OpenAI is the AI vendor itself — selling "AI training" into core engineering/research is implausible; any fit is narrowly enablement/governance for non-technical functions or the OpenClaw ecosystem. Lead with curiosity, not a pitch.
- He is a low-ego-on-bureaucracy, builder-type ("might have built a huge company but it's not exciting for me"); generic vendor outreach will likely be ignored.
